# PRD Quality Review — YouTube Organizer

## Overall verdict
This is a strong, unusually disciplined PRD for a solo brownfield tool: it has a real thesis ("the scrolling **is** the doomscroll"), behavioral success metrics with genuine counter-metrics, an accurately-scoped Non-Goals section, and FRs that mostly carry their own testable consequences. It is correctly shaped as a journey-led capability spec — the four UJs are load-bearing because the product's value is experiential, and the single-user calibration is honest rather than an excuse for vagueness. What's at risk is minor and downstream-facing: a couple of throughput requirements lean on adjectives ("must feel fast") without bounds, one glossary term ("Format") leaks outside the defined Facet set, and there are small ID/roundtrip hygiene gaps that story creation will trip over but not be blocked by.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Decisions are stated as decisions, not smuggled in as "considerations." §10 explicitly tags each platform constraint `(decided)` and separates them from the genuinely-open §11 — R1 (Watch Later inaccessible → `_Inbox` on-ramp) and R2 (no background playback → accept + open-in-YouTube) each name what was given up, not just what was chosen. The write-scope trade-off is surfaced honestly rather than smoothed: FR-1 and R3 both state that write access means "larger consent surface and higher token-handling stakes." `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts land at real tensions — §6.2 flags the pressure-valve decision ("if 100/week triage feels painful even with bulk tools, pull this forward"), and R5 concedes ToS dependency as "acceptable risk for a personal tool" rather than pretending it away. Open Questions are actually open (Watched-clutter longer-term, suggestion-engine mechanics, quota cost) and deferred with reasons, not rhetorical.

## Substance over theater — strong
No persona theater — the single-user framing (§2.2 "Everyone but Alexis") refuses the multi-persona reflex, and the JTBD framing (§2.1) drives actual FRs (Contextual JTBD → FR-13 facet browse; Curatorial → FR-17/FR-18 freshness). The Freshness/Perishable system (§4.7) is earned novelty, not innovation theater — it's traceable to a stated user pain (time-sensitive saves "rotting") and the memlog confirms it as the distinctive feature. NFRs (§8) are product-specific with thresholds tied to metrics ("Bulk operations on ~100 items must feel fast … load-bearing for SM-C2"; "Anti-doomscroll by design") rather than boilerplate "scalable/secure/reliable." The Vision (§1) could not swap into another PRD — it is specific to Watch Later doomscroll and in-app curated playback.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The PRD bets on one thesis and everything follows: YouTube is "only a *source*, not the home of organization," and the doomscroll is the scrolling itself. Feature prioritization follows the thesis, not ease — MVP centers the recurring triage loop (FR-5–FR-7) and the payoff retrieval (FR-13), while suggestions (FR-8) are deferred precisely because they don't yet serve the proven loop. Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity: SM-1 is "opens *the app* … to decide what to watch," not DAU. Counter-metrics are present and sharp — SM-C1 explicitly rejects library size as a success signal ("A bigger library is worse, not better, if it's unwatched") and SM-C2 protects triage throughput. This does not read as a backlog with headings; the four feature groups map cleanly onto the two core loops plus curation.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs carry at least one verifiable consequence, and several are exemplary: FR-6 ("A Video that failed to import is **not** removed from `_Inbox`"), FR-3's dedupe clause ("Re-running import does not create duplicate Videos … Dedupe by YouTube video ID"), and FR-11's precise delete semantics are all directly testable. The weak spots are in the throughput requirements, which is the one place an engineer would have to guess. §8 "Bulk operations on ~100 items **must feel fast**" and FR-7's "without one-at-a-time tedium" state an intent but no bound — no target latency, no items-per-minute, no "single action applies to N selected." Because SM-C2 makes throughput a success/failure axis, leaving it as an adjective is a real gap for story acceptance. UJ-2's climax ("Uncategorized is emptied **or meaningfully reduced**") is similarly soft, though UJs tolerate that better than FRs. FR-16's threshold is honestly flagged as `[ASSUMPTION: ~90%]` rather than left vague, which is the right move.

### Findings
- **medium** Throughput requirement has no measurable bound (§8 Triage throughput; FR-7) — "must feel fast" / "without one-at-a-time tedium" is the load-bearing requirement for SM-C2 yet carries no testable threshold, so "done" for the core triage loop is undefined for story creation. *Fix:* add a bound such as "one bulk action applies to all selected Videos in a single request" plus a target (e.g. "tagging 100 Videos completes in ≤ N interactions / ≤ X seconds").
- **low** Soft climax language in UJ-2 ("emptied or meaningfully reduced") — acceptable in a journey but worth a concrete triage-session target if it informs acceptance. *Fix:* note an expected end-state (e.g. "Uncategorized reaches zero for the week's `_Inbox` catch").

## Scope honesty — strong
Omissions are explicit and do real work. §5 Non-Goals draws the sharp lines the product's framing invites — "Not a YouTube playlist manager," "Not an algorithmic recommender," "Not a … downloader" — each rebutting a plausible misread. §6.2 Out of Scope defers FR-8, native mobile, and background playback with reasons, not silently. Inferences are tagged `[ASSUMPTION: …]` at the point of use and gathered in §9, and the index openly notes that "several earlier assumptions were confirmed by Alexis and are now stated as fact," which is exactly the de-scoping-honestly behavior the rubric rewards. Open-items density (6 indexed assumptions, ~4 open questions, a handful of NOTE FOR PM) is entirely appropriate for a "personal but built for real" tool and does not block a green light.

## Downstream usability — adequate
This PRD feeds UX → architecture → stories, so this dimension matters. The Glossary (§3) is disciplined and the model is well-defined — Library/Video/Uncategorized (as a *derived view*, self-clearing) is stated once and used consistently, which will save architecture real pain. FR IDs are contiguous (FR-1…FR-18), UJ-1…UJ-4 each have a named protagonist (Alexis) carrying context inline, and SM/counter-metric IDs cross-reference the FRs they validate. Cross-references resolve (FR-8 → §6.2, FR-7 referenced from FR-11/FR-17). Two frictions keep it from strong: (1) the term "Format" appears in FR-7 ("apply Tags, **Length/Format**") and §4.4's description but is *not* a Glossary Facet — the Glossary folds format into Tag ("Carries topic *and* format"), so a story author sees a facet that doesn't exist; and (2) Open Questions are numbered 1, 2, 4, 5 — item 3 is missing, a visible ID gap.

### Findings
- **low** "Format" used as if it were a Facet but isn't one (FR-7, §4.4) — Glossary defines Facets as Tag/Channel/Length/Freshness/Dates and folds format into Tag; "Length/Format" implies a distinct attribute story authors will look for and not find. *Fix:* write "Length and Freshness" (or "Tags (topic+format)") to match the Glossary, or add Format as a defined Facet if it is really distinct.
- **low** Open Questions numbering skips 3 (§11) — items run 1, 2, 4, 5, suggesting a dropped question. *Fix:* renumber contiguously.

## Shape fit — strong
Correctly shaped. Although single-user, the product's value is experiential (two core loops), so the journey-led form is right and the UJs are load-bearing rather than overhead — this is the calibration the rubric asks for, not over-formalization. Brownfield references are accurate and distinguished: FR-1/R3 correctly frame OAuth as an "existing foundation" needing a *broader* write scope, and §0 notes the removed `BACKLOG.md` and that stack detail lives in `project-context.md`. Commercial apparatus (market sizing, GTM, multi-persona) is correctly absent, and SMs are appropriately behavioral/operational rather than user-acquisition metrics. Nothing is forced into a shape the product doesn't fit.
